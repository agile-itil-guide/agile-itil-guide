# Agile ITIL Guide

A practical handbook for running IT services the agile way: research, practices, examples, ideas to talk about with teams, AI skills, and AI prompts.

Read it online at <https://agile-itil.github.io>.

## Who this is for

Delivery teams, service desk and operations staff, product owners and service owners, Scrum Masters and agile coaches, IT managers and CIOs, and anyone preparing for agile or ITIL certification who wants practice as well as theory.

## The idea

Agile ways of working (the Agile Manifesto, Scrum, Kanban, Lean, Extreme Programming, DevOps) and IT service management (ITSM), as codified by ITIL, are often presented as rivals: one about speed and change, the other about stability and control. This guide treats them as partners. ITIL 4 already draws on agile, Lean, and DevOps ideas, and agile teams that run live services need incident, change, and asset practices. Each topic shows how to combine them.

## How to use this guide

- **Read a topic** for the core ideas, best practices, a fictional worked example, and four points of view (delivery, operations, leadership, customers).
- **Talk it through.** Every topic has exactly six questions to discuss with your team, each with a short briefing for the facilitator. Topic 6.1 — Team Conversations collects formats for running these conversations.
- **Use the AI prompts.** Each topic has copy-and-paste prompts. Fill in the `[placeholders]` with your own context, and check the answers: the prompts ask the AI to flag its assumptions and never to invent metrics or sources.
- **Use the AI skills.** The [`skills/`](skills/) folder holds skills in Claude Code format that topics link to. See [`skills/README.md`](skills/README.md).
- **Check where you are** with each topic's five-level maturity table (Initiate, Develop, Standardize, Manage, Orchestrate). It is a conversation tool, not a certification.
- **Look things up** in the [glossary](glossary/index.md) and the [index](index/index.md).
