# Agile ITIL Guide — specification

This is the governing specification for the *Agile ITIL Guide*. Where any file in this monorepo disagrees with this document, this document wins: fix the file, or change this document on purpose and bring every affected artifact into line in the same change (see §8).

This repository uses specification-driven development: the spec is written first, `plan.md` says how it will be built, `tasks.md` tracks the build, and every artifact (topic, skill, script, site page) traces back to a section here.

## 1. Product definition

- **Working title:** Agile ITIL Guide
- **Subtitle:** A practical handbook for running IT services the agile way: research, practices, examples, team conversations, AI skills, and AI prompts.
- **Format:** Markdown topics, one directory per topic under `locales/<locale>/topics/<slug>/`, stitched together by `README.md`, `glossary/index.md`, and `index/index.md`, and published as a website by the `agile-itil.github.io/` subproject.
- **Premise:** agile ways of working (the Agile Manifesto, Scrum, Kanban, Lean, Extreme Programming (XP), DevOps) and IT service management (ITSM) as codified by ITIL are often presented as rivals: one about speed and change, the other about stability and control. This guide treats them as complements. ITIL 4 already built on agile, Lean, and DevOps ideas (and ITIL 5, introduced in 2026, continues toward digital product and service management), and agile teams that run live services need incident, change, and asset practices. The guide shows how to combine them in practice.
- **Centre of gravity:** worldwide and vendor-neutral. Teams in commercial companies, the public sector, nonprofits, and managed-service providers are all first-class readers. Named frameworks and tools are examples of patterns, not recommendations.
- **Audience:** delivery teams, service desk and operations staff, product owners and service owners, Scrum Masters and agile coaches, IT managers and CIOs, and anyone getting ready for ITIL or agile certification who wants practice, not just theory.
- **Distinctive features:** every topic includes discussion questions for teams, a fictional worked example, AI prompts the reader can copy and paste, and links to matching AI skills in `skills/`.

## 4. Topic manifest

Numbering is `Part.Topic` and is used in the table of contents, `index/index.md`, `glossary/index.md`, and cross-references. Directory names are slugs only (lowercase, hyphen-separated, generated by `bin/slug-case` from the title, or shortened as listed below) and carry no number, as in `locales/en-001/topics/agile-manifesto/`. Ordering comes from this manifest and `spec/topics.tsv`, not from directory names. The title column below is the plan; once a topic exists, the first headline of its `index.md` is the single source of truth for its title, and every other copy (tables of contents, `llms.*`, cross-references) follows it. New topics are added at the end of their part so existing numbers never change (see §12).

**No preface.** The guide has no preface, foreword, introduction topic, or other front matter: no `preface` directory, no Part 0, and no topic numbered `0.x`. Material a preface would normally hold (who the guide is for, how agile and ITIL fit together, how to use the discussion questions, AI prompts, and skills, and the trademark notice) goes in `README.md`. The guide opens directly with Topic 1.1.

## 5. Voice, style and formatting

Full contract in `STYLE_GUIDE.md`. Key points:

- Practical, calm, and plain. Second person for guidance to the reader; third person for describing practice.
- Expand acronyms on first use *in each topic*, because topics are read on their own.
- 2–4 sentence paragraphs; tables for comparisons and typologies.
- About 2,500–3,500 words of real content per topic, reached by depth, never by padding.
- Cross-references always give number *and* title: "(see Topic 3.4 — Change Enablement)". A bare number is never enough.
- Neutral between frameworks: no "agile good, ITIL bad" (or the reverse). Name the trade-off.
- No hype words: revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, seamless, disruptive, best-in-class.
- Never say "continuous delivery": the phrase is wrong, misleading, and cargo cult. Say "continuous delivery" (or "continuous software delivery"). Agile is a set of values and methods, and delivery is what continuous delivery practices do. The only exception is a proper name or title that contains the words, such as the Disciplined Agile Delivery article, which is quoted as written.
- De-emphasize "sprint", "Scrum", and "Kanban": naming them as the substance of agile is wrong and cargo cult. Emphasize what agile aims for instead: continuous stakeholder involvement, continuous improvement, ongoing evolution of the product and the way of working, a sustainable pace, motivated people, and continuous software delivery. Mention Scrum, sprints, and Kanban only where a topic is specifically about them (Topic 1.5 — Kanban, and the short Scrum section in Topic 1.4 — Delivery Forecasting and Planning Metrics), where a reader may meet them in practice, or where a source names them. Describe them accurately as particular frameworks, never as what agile is.
- Conditional phrasing is the preferred way to mention them: "If your team chooses to use sprints", "If your team chooses to use Scrum", "If your team chooses to use Kanban". The conditional makes clear that the practice is a choice, not a requirement of agile.

## 9. Website subproject: `agile-itil.github.io/`

The reading website lives in `agile-itil.github.io/` inside this monorepo and follows the same architecture as `~/git/public-value-guide/public-value-guide.github.io`, adapted as below.

- **Stack:** SvelteKit 3 with Svelte 5 and `@sveltejs/adapter-static`, prerendering every page to plain HTML; Vite; TypeScript; **pnpm** as the package manager (`pnpm-lock.yaml` and `pnpm-workspace.yaml` committed, with `packageManager` pinned in `package.json`; never use npm or yarn to install). Packages come from the npm registry (npmjs.com).
- **Design system:** Lily Design System packages from npm under `@lilydesignsystem/`: `svelte-headless` (layout, navigation, and content components), `svelte-picker-bar` (the header `PickerBar`: locale, theme, text size, search, and link pickers), and `themes` (theme CSS, synced into `static/assets/themes/` by a `postinstall` script, generic default themes only). The components are headless; all styling lives in `static/assets/style.css`.
- **Internationalization:** Sveltia i18n for UI strings, with one message file per locale in `src/lib/locales/<locale>.json`. `en-001` is canonical; `cy-gb` is the first translation and `zh-cn` the second. Adding a locale needs its message file, one line in `src/lib/locales.ts` (a plain module, because `src/params.ts` loads it in Node), and its synced content. Confirm the exact package name and current version on npmjs.com before installing.
- **Search:** a build-time search index per locale (`build/search-index/<locale>.json`) produced by `scripts/build-search-index.mjs` and queried in the browser through the picker bar's search picker. `/?<query>` is the search URL.
- **Routes:** `/` redirects to the reader's locale (saved choice, else `navigator.language` match, else the `-001` locale for that language, else `en-001`); `/<locale>/contents/`; `/<locale>/topics/<slug>/`; `/glossary/`; `/index/`; `/skills/` (a browsable list of `skills/`); `/sitemap.xml`. Every route is prerenderable.
- **Content:** `scripts/sync-content.sh` copies `../locales` (including each locale's `glossary/index.md` and `index/index.md` where present), `../glossary/index.md`, `../index/index.md`, `../skills`, `../spec/topics.tsv`, `../llms.txt`, and `../llms.json` into `agile-itil.github.io/src/content/` and `static/`. The copy is committed, so the subtree builds on its own without the rest of the monorepo. Never edit `src/content/` by hand.
- **Deployment (git subtree):** the site is published to the repository `agile-itil/agile-itil.github.io` (an organization root Pages site, so `paths.base` stays empty). `bin/deploy-site` runs `git subtree push --prefix=agile-itil.github.io site main`, where `site` is the git remote for that repository. That repository's `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` (kept inside the subtree) installs with `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile` (via `pnpm/action-setup`), runs `pnpm build`, and deploys `build/` with `actions/deploy-pages`. Never push to the site repository directly; all site changes are made here and pushed by subtree.
- **Quality bar:** `pnpm check` reports 0 errors; `pnpm build` completes with no prerender failures; the built `build/` directory served as plain static files has no horizontal scroll at 1440px and 390px, working pickers, and a clean console.
- **Subproject docs:** `agile-itil.github.io/README.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `spec/`  (ported from the public-value-guide site's `spec/lily-design-system-svelte-with-picker-bar/` and `spec/search/`), all subordinate to this spec.

## 11. Review and quality gates

- **Structural gate:** template sections present, named, and ordered; six questions; four perspectives in order; five-column maturity table; checklist, prompt, and skill counts in range; `README.md` symlink valid.
- **Link gate:** every Wikipedia link resolves and appears in `## References`; 5–12 per topic; every skill link resolves.
- **Source gate:** no invented citations, URLs, statistics, or quotations; no long reproduction of copyrighted framework text.
- **Consistency gate:** first headline is the title and cross-references use it exactly; cross-references name the right topic; en-001 conventions followed; acronyms expanded; `.locale-peer-id` present and unique per topic.

`bin/check` automates the structural gate and the offline parts of the link and consistency gates. Report findings as `file — gate — section — what's wrong — what passing looks like`, most severe first (invented source > broken link > structural violation > consistency slip).

## 12. Structural change and renumbering

1. Decide the final manifest before touching any file.
2. Commit before any bulk change so it can be undone.
3. Because directory names carry no numbers, renumbering changes only this manifest, `spec/topics.tsv`, `README.md`, `index/index.md`, `glossary/index.md`, and cross-references; renaming a slug also moves the directory (with its `.locale-peer-id` unchanged) and updates every link to it.
4. Apply changes from one old→new lookup table in a single pass, then run `bin/check`.

## 13. Reference-matter conventions
